# What the numbers mean

Ledger plans dated cash movements at least six calendar months ahead. Confirmed cash, expected income, bills, daily budgets, groceries, pending purchases, and monthly cushion contributions affect the result. Forecasts depend on your records.

| Number | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Bills | Planned obligations, including relevant housing/debt payments. |
| Daily spending | Repeating everyday budget, separate from groceries and discretionary purchases. |
| Groceries | Repeating grocery budget, including pending recorded purchases. |
| Discretionary plan | Forecast total after protections, not permission to spend it immediately. |
| Biweekly allowance | Current period's conservative allocation less recorded discretionary spending. |
| Safety net | Emergency cash actually recorded, not forecast contributions. Earmarked existing cash, not another asset. |

The allowance engine tests a common biweekly allocation against every dated balance. It protects essentials, the actual cushion plus scheduled growth, future allocations, and an additional full allocation of breathing room. It releases 90% of the sustainable allocation and retains 10% within discretionary planning. Refreshing does not reset spending. Unspent cash remains in the household and can improve future capacity; growth is not guaranteed.

Monthly cushion contributions appear in Due Soon, adapt to the actual balance and contributions already recorded that month, cap the final deposit, and stop at the goal. Checking a deposit records an earmark; Ledger does not transfer bank funds.

Record a purchase once. If it has not reduced the checking balance you entered, leave the balance acknowledgement unchecked. It stays pending and reserved. When your balance includes it, use the explicit acknowledgement to avoid subtracting it twice. Its category and planned date keep grocery/daily checklists consistent.

Record the lender's actual mortgage due date and grace deadline. The normal due date remains the target; a supported grace date can resolve a dated shortfall. Ledger cannot verify lender terms or waive fees.

Missing essentials, stale checking information, unknown emergency balances, unconfirmed payments, and dated shortfalls can reduce or block allowance. Zero means no discretionary money is released; it does not mean your finances are comfortable.
